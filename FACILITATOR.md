# Joe's Pizza: Facilitator Guide

A hands-on game for the **Business 101: How Joe's Pizza costs a product to make a profit** deck. Each person runs Joe's kitchen for three Fridays, then sees what their choices did to cost, capacity and profit.

**Time:** about 45 minutes with the deck (about 15 minutes of play in total).
**Group:** any size. Everyone plays on their own laptop.

---

## Before the session

- **Send the link** (see README) a day early and ask people to open it once to check it loads.
- **Laptops with a keyboard** work best. A mouse works too. Phones work but feel cramped.
- **Sound is off** by default, which suits a meeting room.
- **Progress saves in each person's browser.** Players can leave between Fridays with "Save and exit to menu" and pick up where they left off. Nothing is sent anywhere, so you won't see their results unless they share them.
- Play a full run yourself first. It takes about 15 minutes.

## Questions for each debrief step

**1. Predict** ("What did your pizza cost?")
- Who guessed close to $7? Why do most people guess low?
- The pizza didn't change. What did?

**2. Find the leaks** (the profit bridge and the bottleneck)
- What was your biggest bar? Was it ingredients or time?
- What was your bottleneck? Would working faster at the other steps have helped?
- Where are the bottlenecks on our own lines today?

**3. Payday** ("Where does the money come from?")
- Which source did you pick: savings, the bank, or the oven? Why?
- What does "delaying the oven" look like in our business?

**4. Pick one fix**
- Which fix did you expect to be worth the most? Which actually was?
- What's the equivalent lever on your line?

**Floor events** (Fridays 2 and 3)
- Who called the customer early? Who pushed on? How did it turn out?
- "Bad news early is cheaper than bad news late." Where has that been true for us?

**Close-out**
- Put a real product from your area into the calculator. What does the gap cost per week?

## What "good" looks like

| | Joe's standard | Typical first Friday | Strong third Friday |
|---|---|---|---|
| Run rate | 20 an hour | 10–14 an hour | 17–21 an hour |
| Cost per pizza | $7.00 | $8.50–$10.00 | $7.00–$7.80 |
| Profit on 10 pizzas | $20.00 | a loss, or a few dollars | $10–$20 |
| Bottleneck | The oven | Prep (the player) | The oven |

Most people lose money on Friday 1 because they promise from 20 an hour and can't run it yet. That's the point: it sets up slide 22, "Now break the assumption."

## Common questions

**"I made a profit, so why does Payday say Joe is short?"**
Joe's plan is built on standard: $2 a pizza, or $20 a Friday, goes toward a new oven. Running below standard still leaves a profit, but less, so the plan comes up short and the difference has to come from somewhere. That's slide 16: every unnecessary dollar of cost has to come from somewhere.

**"Why was the oven my bottleneck when I was going fast?"**
Two oven decks top out a little above 20 an hour. Once you're quick at prep, the oven sets the pace, and pizzas you start early just pile up ("Made but not needed: overproduction").

**"Why does calling the customer early cost money?"**
It costs 5% off the order. Pushing on and missing costs overtime plus up to 25% off. Bad news early is cheaper than bad news late.

**"Why are the dollar amounts small?"**
They're scaled to a 10-pizza order. The deck's examples use bigger numbers, but the per-pizza math is identical: $12, $7, $5, $2.

**"How do I start over?"**
From the main menu, choose "Start a new run". It asks before erasing anything.
